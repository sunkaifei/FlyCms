# Aesthetic directions

Vocabulary for the plan in step 5 of the rewrite. The goal is to replace "no decision" with one clear point of view that comes from the subject.

**These are words, not a menu.** Picking a style from a list is how a skill grows its own slop: twelve named looks become twelve new defaults. Derive the direction from the product's world first, then use this file to name and sharpen the moves. If you notice you chose a direction before you asked what the product is made of, start again.

## Ground it in the subject

Answer these before choosing anything. Distinctive choices come from the subject's own materials and vernacular; a design for a children's toy and a design for a bond-trading desk should not share a single move.

1. **What is it, and for whom?** One concrete subject, one audience, the page's primary job.
2. **What is its world made of?** Materials, textures, tools, places, the era it belongs to.
3. **What does its world already print?** Signage, packaging, manuals, forms, receipts, labels, maps, uniforms. Its type and color often come from here.
4. **What is the most characteristic thing in that world?** Open with it, in the form that shows it best: a live demo, a real screen, a number that matters, an image, a headline.
5. **What would a competitor's page do?** Whatever that is, it is the default. Do something else on purpose.

Then write the plan: 4-6 named colors with roles, the faces and their roles, the layout idea in one sentence, and one signature detail. Spend the boldness in one place.

## How to commit

1. Answer the grounding questions.
2. Name the defining moves before you write code: **type, palette, layout, motion, one signature detail.** Borrow words from the directions below if they fit; blend two deliberately if that is what the subject asks for. Do not hedge across three.
3. Review the plan against the second-order defaults (catalog SD1-SD8). For each move, ask whether you would have made it for any similar page. Revise the ones that fail and say what changed.
4. Calibrate intensity to the artifact. A landing page can be loud; a settings panel should be quiet and exact.

**Vary across runs, never within a project.** The fastest route to the skill's own slop is the same "safe distinctive" move every time. Anthropic's `frontend-design` skill now lists the five places AI design gathers: cream with a terracotta accent (the Claude look, near `#F4F1EA` and `#D97757`), near-black with one acid-green or vermilion accent, the broadsheet (hairlines, zero radius, dense columns), the SaaS card kit, and template chrome (all-caps eyebrows, `·` meta strings, mono data labels, `→` links). Several directions below sit one step from those places; each carries a **Watch for** line naming its trap. Treat repetition across projects as a tell. Inside one project, the opposite holds: one system, every page.

Fonts named below are examples of a kind of face, not picks. Never copy a named face into a plan; find the one the subject calls for.

## 1. Brutalist / raw
**Fits:** dev tools, indie products, anything that wants to feel honest and un-corporate.
**Type:** a single strong grotesque (Helvetica Now, Aktiv Grotesk) or a mono (Berkeley Mono, Commit Mono). Big, tight, confident.
**Palette:** high contrast. Black on off-white, or one loud primary. Minimal color.
**Layout:** visible grid, hard edges, no rounded corners, exposed structure. Borders over shadows.
**Motion:** little to none, or instant/snappy.
**Signature:** oversized type, a raw `1px` border system, unstyled-but-intentional.
**Watch for:** hairlines plus zero radius plus dense columns is the broadsheet cluster (SD3); a mono face on small labels is template chrome (SD4). Raw has to come from the product's own structure, not from the kit.

## 2. Editorial / magazine
**Fits:** content products, writing tools, brands with a voice.
**Type:** a serif/sans contrast done fully. Display serif (Fraunces, GT Sectra, Tiempos Headline) with a clean body sans (Untitled, Söhne).
**Palette:** paper and ink, one accent. Restrained.
**Layout:** asymmetric editorial grid, pull quotes, drop caps, generous measure, off-center compositions.
**Motion:** subtle, typographic.
**Signature:** real typographic hierarchy, columns, a masthead feel.
**Watch for:** this is the most crowded direction. Cream paper plus a high-contrast serif plus a terracotta accent is SD1, the Claude look; rules and columns on a non-publication are SD3; a one-word serif italic is T3/SD5. Commit to editorial only when the content is editorial, and take the paper and accent from the subject, not from "warm."

## 3. Swiss / International typographic
**Fits:** data products, dashboards, anything that values clarity.
**Type:** one neutral grotesque at several weights (Neue Haas Grotesk, Inter *only if* used with real rigor).
**Palette:** mostly monochrome, one signal color used only for emphasis.
**Layout:** a strict modular grid, flush-left, mathematical spacing, lots of whitespace as structure.
**Motion:** minimal, functional.
**Signature:** discipline. The grid is the design.
**Watch for:** "neutral ground plus one signal" slides into SD1 (on cream, with red-orange) or SD2 (on near-black). Choose the signal for what it signals in this domain, and let the grid, not the accent, carry the identity.

## 4. Retro-futuristic / Y2K / synth
**Fits:** creative tools, music, gaming, launches that want energy.
**Type:** a technical or display face with character (Departure Mono, a wide sans, a chrome display).
**Palette:** dark base with electric accents, or saturated period color. CRT, chrome, neon, used coherently (not the AI "purple glow").
**Layout:** HUD framing, scanlines, grids with depth.
**Motion:** glitch, flicker, terminal-typing, used with restraint.
**Signature:** a coherent era reference, not a sticker pile.
**Watch for:** near-black with one neon is SD2; purple glows are C1/C5. Pick a specific era and source, and use its real palette.

## 5. Organic / natural
**Fits:** wellness, food, sustainability, calm products.
**Type:** humanist sans (Hanken Grotesk, Mr Eaves) or a soft serif.
**Palette:** earth tones, muted greens and clays, warm neutrals. No pure white, no pure black.
**Layout:** soft asymmetry, generous negative space, hand-placed feel.
**Motion:** slow, eased, breathing.
**Signature:** texture (grain, paper), organic shapes that are specific rather than the AI 3D blob.
**Watch for:** clay on cream is SD1 again, and grain or hand-drawn marks added "to look human" are the forming third-order default. Texture has to be the subject's own (its soil, its packaging, its paper), not a filter.

## 6. Luxury / refined
**Fits:** premium brands, finance, high-end services.
**Type:** a high-contrast serif (Canela, GT Super) with a quiet sans.
**Palette:** deep, restrained. One rich dark, warm metallics or a single jewel tone. Lots of space.
**Layout:** centered can work here when it reads as poise, not default. Wide margins, small confident type, slow reveal.
**Motion:** slow fades, deliberate.
**Signature:** restraint and space as a flex. Nothing rushed.
**Watch for:** a high-contrast serif on warm cream is SD1; slow fades on every section are M1. Luxury comes from the material and the edit, not the serif.

## 7. Playful / toy
**Fits:** consumer apps, kids, social, anything joyful.
**Type:** a rounded or characterful display (a chunky rounded sans), generous weight.
**Palette:** bright, confident, a real color story (not the timid even spread, see catalog C4).
**Layout:** bouncy, overlapping elements, stickers, depth, big touch targets.
**Motion:** springy, bouncy, reactive.
**Signature:** personality, mascots or marks with a point of view.
**Watch for:** springy motion everywhere is M4; stock sticker packs are generated filler. The personality has to belong to this product.

## 8. Art deco / geometric
**Fits:** events, hospitality, premium consumer.
**Type:** a geometric display (a deco-influenced face), tall and elegant.
**Palette:** two or three colors with metallic or deep contrast.
**Layout:** symmetry done *intentionally*, strong geometric motifs, framing lines, repeated patterns.
**Motion:** elegant, geometric reveals.
**Signature:** a repeated geometric motif that becomes the brand.

## 9. Industrial / utilitarian / technical
**Fits:** infrastructure, B2B, engineering tools.
**Type:** a mono or a technical grotesque, small and dense.
**Palette:** greyscale with one functional accent, status colors that mean something.
**Layout:** dense, table-driven, information-first, tight spacing where density helps.
**Motion:** near none.
**Signature:** labels, technical readouts, a spec-sheet feel. Density as honesty.
**Watch for:** mono data labels, `·` meta strings, and all-caps readouts are exactly the template chrome of SD4. Use them only where a reader actually scans data; everywhere else, plain labels in the body face.

## 10. Maximalist
**Fits:** culture, fashion, bold launches, portfolios.
**Type:** mix display faces with intent, large scale jumps.
**Palette:** many colors, but composed, not timid. Clashing on purpose.
**Layout:** dense, layered, overlapping, scrolling surprises, grid-breaking.
**Motion:** lots, but choreographed.
**Signature:** controlled chaos. Every loud element is a decision.

## 11. Warm minimal (done well)
**Fits:** most SaaS, when the brand wants calm and trustworthy.
**Type:** one good humanist sans, paired with a quiet accent face.
**Palette:** a considered light ground, a true ink, one confident accent. Never grey-on-grey.
**Layout:** clear hierarchy, real whitespace rhythm, left-aligned, asymmetric where it helps.
**Motion:** one orchestrated entrance, restrained micro-interactions.
**Signature:** the details, exact spacing, real component states, considered type scale.
**Watch for:** this is the closest direction to both orders of default. Off-white plus a warm accent is SD1; soft cards are K2. It only works with real precision and a palette traced to the subject. If you reach for it by reflex, you have not chosen.

## 12. Monospace / terminal
**Fits:** dev tools, CLIs, technical brands.
**Type:** a mono throughout (Berkeley, JetBrains, Commit), paired with a minimal sans if needed.
**Palette:** terminal-inspired but considered: a light ground with a single ink, or a dark theme built as a full palette.
**Layout:** text-driven, ASCII structure, boxes, alignment to a character grid.
**Motion:** typing, cursor blinks, used sparingly, and off under reduced-motion.
**Signature:** the medium is the message. Real terminal craft, not a green-text gimmick.
**Watch for:** near-black with a phosphor green is SD2, and a fake window with traffic-light dots is SD7. Earn the terminal with the product's real commands and output.

## Quick reference

Use this to find words for a direction you already derived, not to choose one.

- **Dev tool / honest**: Brutalist, Industrial, Monospace
- **Content / voice**: Editorial, Maximalist
- **Data / clarity**: Swiss, Industrial
- **Premium**: Luxury, Art deco
- **Consumer / joyful**: Playful, Organic
- **Calm SaaS**: Warm minimal (only if executed precisely), Organic

When two fit, blend deliberately (e.g. editorial type on a Swiss grid). When none fits, that is a good sign: follow the subject and name the result yourself.
